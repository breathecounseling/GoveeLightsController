A show is a sequence of presets with transitions between them: a wipe across the house, a rise up the gable, a flash… It takes over, then hands back to whatever was playing.

Showtimes

Run a show automatically, e.g. every hour on the hour from 5 to 10 pm, or only on one date (12-31). Normally a showtime only plays while the lights are on (schedule or Live); untick that and it switches them on for the show, then off again.

Security

Output

Gamma 1.0 = off; ~2.2 if mid colours look washed out on the bulbs.

Only turn on "re-enable every" if re-sending realtime doesn't make your boxes blink (try Enable realtime twice in diagnostics).

Map the bulbs with your camera

Makes 2D effects (ripples, sweeps, pictures, fire rising) line up with the real roofline. The lights blink a code, you film them for ~25 seconds, and your phone works out where every bulb is. Nothing is uploaded.

Colour-match the runs

Different runs can tint slightly differently. Show plain white on everything, then lower red, green or blue on any run that looks off until they all match (for example, less blue for a warmer white).

Hologram fans

Black is see-through on a fan: only the bright parts float in the air.

Display

Any shape works: the scenes fill the width. On a projector, black is simply no light, so only Santa and the snow land on the wall.

Each LED is the average of the picture it covers, so thin lines turn dim rather than vanish. The "raw RGB" file is the frames byte for byte, for WLED over DDP; to play it live, see the tips below.

Fans

The gap between neighbouring fans' blade tips, as mounted. Splice mode: each fan plays its own slice of one big picture, so measure it.

Make the files

Projector or screen

  • Projector on the house: play the MP4 on a loop (most media players and many projectors' USB players loop a file). Black is no light, so keep the background black and aim at a light wall or a white sheet in a window.
  • TV or monitor in a window: pick the night-sky background, or keep black for a "floating" look on an OLED.
  • Size: make it the projector's own resolution (1920 × 1080 for most), so nothing is rescaled.

LED matrix

  • WLED: set the panel up under Config → 2D, then either upload the small GIF (recent WLED builds with GIF support play it with the Image effect) or stream it live from the PC (experimental): python -m goveelights hologram santa-landing --matrix 64x32 --live <WLED IP>
  • Raw RGB: width × height × 3 bytes a frame, frames back to back, rows from the top left (or zig-zag); the .json file next to it gives the size and frame rate.
  • LED-look GIF: just to check how it will look from the street.

Loading them onto the fans

  • One file per fan. Each fan gets its own clip (fan 1 is the left one; on a square of four: top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right). Upload it in the fan's own app (Wi-Fi or Bluetooth) or copy it to the fan's SD card, then set the fan to loop it.
  • MP4 if the app takes it, GIF otherwise. Most apps accept a square MP4 (720 × 720 is safe) and re-encode to the fan's own resolution.
  • Splice (a row or square of fans): start the clips together (most apps have a "sync" or group play). If yours can't, the scenes still read on each fan alone.
  • No live control. No consumer fan takes a live picture from a PC; switching clips is done in the fan's app. (Experimental community protocols exist for some models; see docs/HOLOGRAM_FANS.md.)
  • Safety: the blades are fast and hard. Mount out of reach of children and passers-by, behind the clear guard or dome; outdoors use an IP65 (rain-rated) domed fan and an outdoor-rated plug. Fix it so wind can't swing it into anything, and check the maker's cold-weather limit before winter nights.

Set up your lights

A few quick steps. You can change all of it later.

1. Find your light boxes

In the Govee app, turn on LAN Control for each box, and keep this PC on the same Wi-Fi. Then search. Give each box a name and its number of bulbs (the Govee app shows it), in the order they run along the house.

Not found? Add one by its IP address

2. Where is the house?

Only used to work out sunset and the sky colours, on this PC. Two decimals (about 1 km) is plenty.

3. Follow a team (optional)

Game-day celebrations and a game-day look in the holiday calendar. Leave empty to skip.
